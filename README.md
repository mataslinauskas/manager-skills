# Manager skills

A collection of skills that coach you through the toughest challenges of leadership.

They'll help you with getting buy-in, solving complex problems, giving feedback, delegating and more.

Compiled from my experience building [Creo](https://www.joincreo.com/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=manager-skills&utm_content=intro) and coaching executives from the University of California, Coursera, Vinted and others.

## Why use them?

I've designed them to help you adopt the thinking of top managers.

Instead of giving you the answers, these skills lead you through questions that an experienced leader would focus on.

This way, if you go through them consistently, you start thinking like one.

## How each skill works

### buy-in-prep

![The buy-in map filling in, card by card](assets/buy-in-prep.gif)

Use it before you ask for budget, approval or people's time. The coach takes you through 6 questions:

1. What do you want to get buy-in for, and what's the specific yes you need?
2. Who has to agree?
3. What does each person care about most, and how do you know?
4. Which of their problems would your initiative solve, and what proves they feel them?
5. How does it solve them, and what backs that up?
6. What's the smallest test that would prove it works?

As you answer, it builds a **buy-in map**: one card per person, showing what's in place and what's still missing before you pitch them. When you're done, the map's **Present** view gives you a one-page pitch for each person to show in the meeting.

You can go step by step, or describe your situation and get the full plan in one go.

Start a new chat and say what you need buy-in for, for example: "I want to get approval for a pilot with my team." The coach takes it from there.

### progress-next-steps

![Setting up weekly updates for a manager, then the first update: reading the sources and drafting the email](assets/progress-next-steps.gif)

Use it when your manager, a client or anyone else expects regular updates from you. Setup takes 3 questions:

1. Who do you want to give updates to, and who are they to you?
2. What do they want to know from you?
3. How often should you update them, and where?

It then shows the sources it will read for that person, such as your past AI chats, Slack, Teams, email and calendar, and you can add or remove any. If your app can run scheduled tasks, it sets one up for each person. If not, it tells you which day to ask for the update.

On each update, it reads what happened since the last one and lists what you got done, with where it found each item. It asks for your next steps, then drafts the update in that person's format. Any change you make to the format is kept for next time.

It never sends anything. You check the draft and send it yourself.

Start a new chat and say who you want to keep up to date, for example: "I want to send my manager a weekly update." It takes it from there.
